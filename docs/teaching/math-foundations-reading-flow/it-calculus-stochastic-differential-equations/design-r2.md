### Canonical coverage and scope decisions

Fehrman's complete contents and the selected integration/formula/existence/change-of-measure chapters, Higham's entire numerical paper, the original Itô paper at its readable scope and Goodman generator sections supply complementary treatments. Actual reference pictures inspected are Higham's common-path comparison and stability plane. Use their teaching jobs in new geometry, not copied data or misleading labels. A complete original integration theorem or general semimartingale decomposition would exceed this lesson: retain the exact Brownian construction and proof outline. Local time appears as the explicit boundary for absolute value, not a promised implementation. Tanaka counterexamples and the strong/weak existence equivalence theorem would need substantial new machinery; name no such theorem without teaching it. Rough paths, stochastic Runge–Kutta, Lévy-area simulation, adaptive SDE solvers, non-Gaussian driving jumps and general Girsanov remain annotated further directions. Hutzenthaler–Jentzen–Kloeden supplies a precise warning about superlinear explicit Euler, not a new large rare-event experiment. Preserve stochastic-voltage and forward-corruption applications with their model assumptions.

No observed dataset is required: this topic constructs and checks declared mathematical processes, rather than fitting a method to observations. The quantities are analytic, finite constructed traces or explicitly seeded synthetic samples. Adding an arbitrary measurement file would obscure those comparisons. The diagnostic application explains what observed-data evidence would be needed without claiming such evidence exists.
