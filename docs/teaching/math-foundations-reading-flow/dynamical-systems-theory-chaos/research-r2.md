## Source disagreements resolved before writing

These are checks on mathematical statements, not reasons to discard otherwise useful sources.

- Menon's forward-invariance definition prints equality of sets; our forward-invariant contract is inclusion for nonnegative time. A solution may enter a smaller subset.
- Menon's gradient chapter has a missing minus sign in the Hessian linearization of negative gradient flow; use `A=−D²V`. Its absolute-value growth remark does not alone ensure bounded sublevels: use `V(x)→+∞`, or directly assume a compact positively invariant sublevel.
- Menon's return-map prose cannot mean that every nearby point returns to itself; the next oriented crossing is generally a different point. The variational fundamental matrix satisfies `Y′=B(t)Y`, with the derivative present.
- Romik's tent/logistic coordinate map names the inverse of our `h`. Define `h(y)=sin²(πy/2)` locally; its inverse is the CDF. Put the factor `2/π` on the entire CDF difference. The doubling map has the fixed point0 under the `[0,1)` convention despite an incidental contrary sentence in the notes.
- Rothman's saddle-node paragraph uses the wrong parameter sign beside `√μ`, and a marginal radial sentence prints a square where the stated field gives a cube. Substitute into the original equations. An unstable periodic point with a positive exponent prevents the shorthand positive-exponent-equals-chaos inference.
- Tedrake's scalar decay condition should be accompanied by norm-comparison bounds for an exponential state estimate. We use local quadratic upper/lower bounds when asserting exponential stability by a Lyapunov function. Global radial unboundedness supplies confinement, not those comparison constants automatically.
- Negative divergence contracts volume; Lorenz boundedness uses a separate trapping argument, not divergence alone. This lesson retains the divergence calculation and explicitly declines a full attractor proof.

## Concept research: seven questions answered

Each row states why the concept exists; a worked mechanism; why it works; failure boundary; alternative; real implementation difference; and useful continuing practice. These are author decisions from the actual readings plus local derivation, not quotations.

| Concept | Seven-question synthesis |
| --- | --- |
| Complete state and clock | Need a rule that can choose a future. Equal cart position with opposite velocity gives opposite motion. State retains the information on which the rate depends; explicit forcing also needs input/time. Missing memory or nonunique rate laws break that contract. Use augmented states or input-driven systems. Measurements are often projections and estimated states carry error. Practice distinguishing omitted velocity, forcing and stochastic innovation before choosing a model. |
| Flow versus map | Need to tell a rate from an update. Cooling `x′=−kx` accumulates to `e^(−kh)x`, whereas Euler uses `(1−kh)x`. Differentiation verifies the flow and semigroup composition. Large steps can change stability; local uniqueness does not ensure global lifetime (`x′=x²`). Exact sampling or adaptive integration are alternatives. Libraries choose internal steps and expose termination. Practice equal-horizon refinement and explicit units. |
| Stability and linearization | Need to know what small disturbances do. Compare zero, negative cubic, positive cubic, and negative linear fields. Signs/exact solutions distinguish staying near from attraction and rates. Derivative zero or map multiplier±1 leaves strict linear tests undecided. Use nonlinear sign or Lyapunov reasoning. Finite classification needs tolerance and admissible state domain. Practice boundary counterexamples rather than inventing an equality rule. |
| Basins, potential and bifurcation | Need global destinations beyond local slope. In `a x−x³`, arrows carry positive/negative starts into different wells; roots change with a. Integrating `−f` gives `V̇=−f²`. One minimum's local certificate is not a global guarantee; folds are one-sided. Use phase-line signs or sublevel confinement. Parameter continuation and finite-rate sweep differ. Practice changed coefficient4 and tilted folds, keeping parameter axes distinct from time. |
| Lyapunov and invariant sets | Need long-run conclusions without solving every orbit. Shift the well's potential to zero, trap a small sublevel, follow decrease. Compact forward invariance plus LaSalle identifies where a trajectory can remain with zero decrease. Nonpositive derivative may leave an entire invariant set, not one point. Use exact solutions, strict decrease or a suitable invariant-set argument. Certificate searches have numerical residuals and domain constraints. Practice separating sampled negativity from an all-state certificate. |
| Phase portraits and transient transfer | Need geometry hidden by one scalar trace. A center rotates `(q,p)` while the triangular stable flow transfers decaying p into q. Exact formulas and `e^(tA)` verify it. Eigenvalues omit short-time norm growth and coordinate scaling. Singular-value/norm analysis complements eigenmodes. Libraries avoid assuming a complete eigenbasis. Practice `(1,0)` versus `(0,1)` and ordered changing matrices. |
| Cycles, Hopf and return sections | Need an attracting repeating set rather than an attracting point. Radial `r′=ar−r³`, angular `θ′=1` gives radius√a and return multiplier`e^(−4πa)`. Linearizing radial error over2π explains attraction; phase stays neutral. The origin stays put, and generic return times vary. Poincaré sections reduce dimension; planar trapping can prove existence. Event code needs crossing direction and an initial-event exclusion. Practice phase offset and a changed section. |
| Logistic iteration and bifurcation atlas | Need repeated output-as-input reasoning. Draw `.2→.4→.6` at r2.5, then a two-cycle at3.2. Chain rule gives product derivative, not either point's derivative alone. Short tails/rounded points cannot prove a period; intervals contain windows and exceptional starts. Exact factorization or residual checks supplement sampled diagrams. Bounded atlas sampling discards a stated transient and can miss thin windows. Practice changed r with explicit evidence strength. |
| Sensitivity and exponents | Need a local error-growth quantity. Compose derivatives along one reference and sum natural logs, compare to an actual pair. Chain rule explains tangent growth before nonlinear saturation. Zero derivative means a singular log term; a positive exponent can belong to a fixed orbit. Renormalized tangent vectors/QR are alternatives in more dimensions. Real measurements add noise and state reconstruction assumptions. Practice repairing a classifier and qualifying a finite forecast horizon. |
| Stretch/fold and symbolic cylinders | Need a mechanism establishing more than an irregular plot. A tent branch stretches by2; inverse L/R contracts by1/2. Composing inverse branches gives width2^−n and a repeating point by contraction. Endpoints can have dual itineraries and minimal period can divide word length. Logistic4 is an equivalent coordinate view, not an identical density. Binary grids eventually repeat and can collapse to zero. Practice RL versus LR with exact fractions and boundary labels. |
| Invariance and typical statistics | Need useful long-run quantities when detailed forecasts fail. Two preimages of an interval each have half its length; map uniform tent mass through h. Conservation of interval mass gives the CDF/density. Invariance alone is not ergodicity and exceptions are real. Transfer operators or density evolution generalize the construction. Empirical histograms depend on start, arithmetic, burn and binning. Practice an interval probability and an exceptional exact periodic orbit. |
| Lorenz and projection | Need a three-dimensional counterpoint to planar flow. Follow one full `(x,y,z)` in two projections and calculate equilibria/divergence. Local uniqueness applies in full state, not each projection. A butterfly shape, local tolerance or shrinking volume is no chaos/physical-validity proof. Short-time independent integrators and declared statistics answer narrower questions. Production simulations track failures, units and observables. Practice an evidence plan for a learned simulator. |
| Numerical structure | Need to distinguish dynamics of an equation from dynamics of its algorithm. Forward Euler expands oscillator energy; kick then drift preserves a modified quadratic form. Algebra shows the update matrix/invariant. At h2 the form is degenerate and cannot bound the state. Smaller steps, implicit midpoint or Verlet address different goals. Symplecticity is not exact ordinary energy, accuracy or universal stability. Practice implementing update order and the h2 witness. |
| Optimization, recurrence and learned models | Need a transfer to algorithms. A quadratic gradient step has the same multiplier as Euler; A then B redirects a perturbation despite each matrix's zero spectrum. Chain rule and negative-gradient energy identity explain both. Nonconvex basins and off-distribution rollouts defeat local/one-step claims. Structure-aware learned dynamics and rollout validation are alternatives. New research on learned certificates and geometric models needs its own data/assumption validation. Practice a changed matrix product and a measurement-aware validation plan; no unverified current-best-model claim. |
