## Design Context

### Site-wide personal hub
On 23 September the user chose a personal home with clear Portfolio and Learn entrances. `/` introduces Ronak and connects the site; the preserved portfolio lives at `/portfolio`. Keep a calm typographic, black-and-amber editorial presentation. The shared section switcher separates global destinations from local learning navigation. Articles is now an explicitly requested destination with an honest empty state until real writing is supplied. Future research notes and deployed tools may become distinct destinations when real content exists. Follow [the site architecture](docs/engineering/SITE-ARCHITECTURE.md) and [repository boundaries](docs/engineering/REPOSITORY-STRUCTURE.md); avoid fake previews, dead tabs and importing the full curriculum into the home page.

On 10 October the user approved a redesigned Home. It leads with the name, then a motto in which only "training it" resolves from noise. Below that are a route finder over the real prerequisite graph, with an optional whole-curriculum index, and a Work section with on-site projects and external links. See [HOME-DESIGN.md](docs/engineering/HOME-DESIGN.md). The user rejected a dense map of every lesson as cluttered. Prefer one clear thing per section over showing the size of the data. Home is the first part of a site-wide redesign; Portfolio, Learn and Articles follow in the same language.

### Users
The learning hub serves a broad technical audience: complete beginners learning fundamentals, experienced professionals switching into AI/ML, and practitioners or researchers who need a reliable place to learn new material or systematically revise a field. Visitors need to find an appropriate starting point quickly, understand prerequisites, and make steady progress through a very large curriculum.

The 22 September direction adds a stronger research workflow: connect deep concepts to runnable end-to-end projects, compare baselines, investigate failure cases and produce reproducible evidence. Beginners still need clear routes; research orientation must not turn the entry point into jargon or a wall of paper titles.

### Brand Personality
Technically fluent, encouraging, and intellectually serious. The Learn experience extends the portfolio's "training loop" personality: knowledge is distilled, progress is visible, and the interface rewards curiosity without making newcomers feel excluded.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the existing near-black background, neutral charcoal surfaces and amber identity accent. The user explicitly rejected the green/olive redesign on 22 September: do not introduce green-tinted surfaces, borders, secondary text or progress accents. Use the shared `--learn-*` palette in `src/learn/learning-workspace.css` for the workspace and projects. This supersedes the previous “green for progress” direction. Avoid generic course-marketplace cards and loud AI-neon treatments. The content hierarchy should feel calm and editorial even when the underlying subject matter is dense.

### Design Principles
1. Orient learners before presenting complexity: paths, goals, prerequisites, and next steps come before exhaustive catalogues.
2. Make curriculum scale legible: group related material, disclose depth progressively, and keep universal search and revision routes close at hand.
3. Be honest about content readiness: distinguish published lessons from planned material while keeping the decided teaching sequence. Planned entries show their outline and actual adjacent topics; publication must not cause a shortcut past them.
4. Support both guided and non-linear study: a beginner can follow a sequence while an expert can jump directly to a precise topic.
5. Preserve the training-loop visual language while maintaining readable contrast, keyboard access, and responsive navigation in the dark-only interface.
6. Use Explore, Paths and Projects as connected entrances. Search spans the curriculum; project stages link to exact prerequisite owners. Add future research papers, tools and lab collections as distinct resources only when real content exists, not disabled promises.
7. Keep project milestone progress separate from lesson completion and from evidence that an experiment succeeded. A useful negative result is part of research learning.
